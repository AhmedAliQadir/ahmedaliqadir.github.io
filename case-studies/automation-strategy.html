From 1,900 Zaps to an automation programme

A mid-size SaaS company had automation everywhere and a strategy nowhere. This is the engagement I would run end to end: discovery and a measured baseline, a scored portfolio of use cases, three orchestrations designed in Zapier, an ROI model the CFO can argue with, and a governance model that lets citizen developers keep building safely.

Customer
Corvanta (fictional) — B2B SaaS, ~450 people
Format
Worked engagement, 2026
My role
Automation strategist — discovery to governance
Platform
Zapier: Zaps, Tables, Forms, Agents, AI steps, Canvas, Admin Center

Read this first. Corvanta is an invented company and the baseline figures are illustrative assumptions, chosen to be realistic for a business of its size and labelled wherever they appear. What is real is the method, the scoring, the orchestration designs and the governance model — the same work I would do with an actual customer, just without their data.

1. The situation

Corvanta sells project-analytics software to mid-market and enterprise teams. Three years on Zapier has produced what bottom-up adoption usually produces: a lot of value and no map of it. About 140 people build Zaps across Sales, Customer Success, Support, Marketing, Finance and People. Nobody can say which automations the business depends on, who owns them, or what they are worth.

The trigger for the engagement was a renewal conversation. The COO's question was not "should we keep Zapier?" but "how do we grow 30% next year without growing headcount at the same rate — and is automation part of that answer or a pile of hidden risk?" That question frames everything below.

~1,900active Zaps, no naming standard

2. Discovery: what they said vs. what they need

Discovery ran in two tracks in the first two weeks: executive alignment with the people who own outcomes, and working sessions with the people who build and live inside the workflows. Most of the value came from the gap between the two.

StakeholderWhat they asked forWhat they actually neededMeasure they care about

CRO"More pipeline."The pipeline they already generate, worked faster. Leads were going cold, not missing.Speed to lead, lead-to-meeting rate

Two findings shaped the rest of the engagement. First, the CRO's pipeline problem was a speed problem: inbound leads waited in a shared inbox for an SDR manager to route them by hand. Second, the Security team's instinct to lock everything down would have been the most expensive decision in the room — it would have cut off the 140 people producing most of the value. The answer was tiers, not a gate.

3. The use-case portfolio

Every candidate automation from discovery went into one portfolio and was scored 1–5 on value (hours, revenue or risk it moves), effort (build, data and change-management cost) and risk (customer-facing exposure, data sensitivity, blast radius if it fails). The weights are a judgement call, so they are adjustable here — the point of the exercise is to make the trade-off explicit, not to hide it in a spreadsheet.

    At the default weights, lead routing and the Sales-to-CS handoff are clear quick wins. The at-risk early warning ranks lower because it is harder and riskier — but it moves the metric the VP of Customer Success is measured on, and it is the use case that shows the business what agents can do with proper guardrails. So it went into wave 1 as a deliberate strategic bet, with tighter controls (section 4). Renewal kickoff and dunning alerts are cheap enough that the champion's team built them in parallel.

    4. Three orchestrations

    Each flagship is designed as a system rather than a single Zap: a Zapier Table holds state and the audit trail, Zaps move work between systems, AI steps handle the judgement that used to sit with a person, and a human approves anything that reaches a customer. Each was mapped in Canvas first so stakeholders could challenge the process before anything was built.

    Trigger AI step or agent Human in the loop Action

    A. Speed-to-lead routing

    Target: median first touch 9.5 h → under 15 min
    1. TNew inbound leadHubSpot form / website

      Fires on every demo request and contact-sales submission.

    2. AIEnrich and classifyAI by Zapier

      Returns structured fields only: company size band, ICP fit (A/B/C), intent (demo, pricing, support, spam) and a one-line reason. Prompt and output schema are versioned.

    3. 1Write to the Lead LedgerZapier Tables

      One row per lead with classification, owner, timestamps and SLA status — the system of record for the metric.

    4. 2Route by PathsPaths · Tables lookup

      Fit A → next AE in the round-robin table. Fit B/C → SDR queue. Support intent → Zendesk ticket and a polite redirect. Spam → archive with reason.

    5. 3Notify the ownerSlack DM · Salesforce

      Lead created or updated in Salesforce with owner set; DM includes the AI summary and a one-click link.

    6. 4SLA watchDelay · Filter

      After 15 minutes, check the ledger. If untouched, escalate to the team channel and reassign.

    Guardrails. The AI step never sends anything to the prospect. Low-confidence classifications default to the SDR queue rather than guessing. Weekly sample of 20 classifications reviewed by the SDR manager for drift.

    B. Sales-to-CS handoff

    Target: 3.5 days → same day; 18 manual steps → 3
    1. TOpportunity moves to Closed WonSalesforce

      Filtered to new-logo deals; expansions follow a lighter path.

    2. 1Create the onboarding recordZapier Tables

      Account, ARR band, products, contacts and promised go-live date — the single place CS, Finance and the AE look at.

    3. AIDraft the handoff briefAI by Zapier

      From opportunity notes and the last call summary: goals in the customer's words, success criteria, risks, stakeholders, anything promised in the sales cycle.

    4. HAE approves the briefHuman in the loop

      The AE corrects or approves in one step. Nothing goes to the customer from an unapproved brief.

    5. 2Assign a CSMTables lookup

      By segment and current CSM load, not by whoever is free in Slack.

    6. 3Spin up the workspaceAsana · Slack · Gmail

      Project from template, shared Slack channel, kickoff email drafted for the CSM to send.

    Guardrails. Customer-facing email is drafted, never sent automatically. The brief is stored with the approver's name, so the handoff is attributable.

    C. At-risk account early warning

    Target: late churn signals 22% → under 5%
    1. TDaily scheduleSchedule by Zapier

      Runs each weekday before the CS stand-up.

    2. 1Collect signalsZendesk · Stripe · warehouse webhook

      Ticket volume and sentiment spikes, failed payments, and a weekly-active-users drop sent from the data warehouse into a Health Signals table.

    3. AGAgent assesses each flagged accountZapier Agents

      Reads the signals and the account's Salesforce record, scores risk with a stated reason, and picks a recommended play from the CS playbook stored in Tables.

    4. 2Post the digestSlack

      One message per CSM: their at-risk accounts, why, and the suggested play.

    5. HCSM decidesHuman in the loop

      Accept, adjust or dismiss — and the decision is written back so the scoring can be tuned.

    Guardrails. The agent is read-only on Salesforce, enforced with Enterprise action restrictions rather than prompt instructions. It recommends, it does not act. Dismissal reasons feed a monthly review of false positives with the VP of Customer Success.

    5. The ROI model

    The model counts only time returned to people, because that is the number a finance team will accept without a fight. Every input is an assumption, so every input is editable.     6. Governance: tiers, not a gate

    The governance model protects the business-critical automations without slowing down the people who create most of the value. Every Zap lives in one of three tiers, and the controls scale with the tier. Each control maps to a feature that Zapier Enterprise already provides, so this is configuration and habit, not new tooling.

    Tier 1

    Personal productivity

    Anyone, self-serve. Approved apps only.

    • App access controls define the approved list
    • Domain restrictions block personal accounts on business apps
    • No review needed
    Tier 2

    Team process

    A team depends on it. Owner and backup owner required.

    • Naming standard and shared team folder
    • Shared app connections, not personal ones
    • Error alerts routed to a team channel
    Tier 3

    Business-critical

    Revenue, customers or regulated data. Reviewed before launch.

    • Managed app connections owned by IT
    • Action restrictions on what AI and agents can write
    • Audit log streamed to the SIEM; documented runbook

    Naming standard: TEAM · Process · Trigger → Outcome · Tier, for example CS · Onboarding · ClosedWon → Workspace · T3. A Zap's name should tell an admin who to call and how worried to be.

    The first governance sprint is remediation, not policy: reassign or retire the 27% of Zaps on deactivated or personal accounts, starting with anything touching Salesforce, billing or customer email. Nothing builds trust in a governance programme like fixing the thing that was quietly about to break.

    Days 0–30Baseline captured. Lead routing live. Ownerless-Zap remediation. Tiers and naming standard published.Median first touch on inbound leads; ownerless Tier 2/3 Zaps at zero.

    Between reviews I would watch the account signals that predict whether the programme is healthy or stalling: active builders month on month, the share of task usage running through Tier 2 and 3 Zaps, error rates on business-critical Zaps, and how many new use cases arrive through the intake form rather than through a crisis. Rising builders with falling errors means the programme is maturing; rising errors on Tier 3 means stop and fix before expanding.

    8. The executive briefing

    The output for the sponsor is one page, written to be read in two minutes and decided on in five.

    To
    COO, Corvanta
    Re
    Automation programme — 90-day plan and decision needed

    The short version

    Corvanta already runs on automation; it just isn't managed as an asset. Three orchestrations return about 440 hours a month — roughly three people's time — and cut lead response from hours to minutes. A tiered governance model removes the risk sitting in 500 unowned Zaps without slowing the 140 people who build them.

    Decisions needed

    • Approve the three-tier governance model and the naming standard.
    • Name a Tier 3 reviewer in IT and give RevOps the authority to retire ownerless Zaps.
    • Agree that the base case counts time saved only, with revenue upside tracked separately.

    Upside not in the base case

    Faster lead response typically lifts lead-to-meeting conversion, and earlier churn warning protects renewals. Both are tracked from day one, and reported once there is a quarter of data.

    9. How I used AI on this

    I built this with Claude as a collaborator, and I think how that went is as relevant as the output.

    • Brief and direction — me. I chose the target (an automation strategist role), the scenario (a mid-size SaaS customer, closest to the businesses that buy Zapier Enterprise) and the rule that the Zaps get built in Zapier by me, not just drawn.
    • Research — Claude, checkable. Claude gathered Zapier's current product line and Enterprise controls. Every control named in section 6 appears on Zapier's own pricing and governance pages.
    • First draft — Claude. The scoring rubric, the three flow designs, the ROI model and this page started as Claude drafts. Claude also proposed labelling the customer as fictional rather than dressing a hypothetical up as a real client, and I kept that: a strategy case study is only worth reading if you can trust what it claims.
    • The bar. Every number is either labelled as an assumption or recalculated in code, and the ROI base case counts time saved only — the conservative case is the one that survives a finance review.

    What this shows

    Automation strategy is mostly not about automation. It is discovering what a customer needs underneath what they ask for, making trade-offs visible, measuring the before so the after means something, and designing controls that let more people build rather than fewer. The Zaps are the easy part.
